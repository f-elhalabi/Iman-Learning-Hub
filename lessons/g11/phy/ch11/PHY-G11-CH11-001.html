Section 2

💡 Warm-Up

Think first, then click to reveal the answer
1. In everyday language, "work" suggests effort and fatigue. Is this the same as the physics definition of work?
No. In physics, work is done only by a force during a displacement — not by a person or an object simply "trying hard." There is no work without both a force and a motion.
2. A weightlifter holds a barbell perfectly still above their head for 30 seconds. Does the weightlifter's force do any work on the barbell, in the physics sense?
No. Since the barbell does not move (displacement = 0), the work done is zero, even though the person feels tired. Effort is not the same as physics work.
3. (Question 11.1) What is the power of a force that is always perpendicular to the velocity of the object it acts on?
Zero. Since P = F·v, and the force is always perpendicular to v, cos 90° = 0, so the power (and the work) of that force is zero at every instant. This is the case for the normal reaction force or tension in circular motion.
4. (Question 11.2) Can you give an example of potential energy of an electric or magnetic nature?
Yes — an electric charge placed inside an electric field has electric potential energy, related to its position relative to other charges. Similarly, a magnet near another magnet has magnetic potential energy that depends on their relative position and orientation.
