🎯 Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1Identify starch, reducing sugar, lipid, and protein in foods.
2Match each food constituent with its experimental test and positive observation.
3Analyze variables, results, and significance in food-test investigations.
4Justify why bread is a complex food using organic and inorganic tests.
Section 2

💡 Warm-Up — Click to Reveal

Why do scientists test food?
Because constituents are often invisible; chemical tests create observable changes.
What does dark blue after iodine indicate?
The presence of starch.
What does a translucent spot indicate?
The presence of lipids/fats.
If Biuret remains blue, what does it mean?
Negative result: protein is not detected.
Section 3

📖 Learning Activity and Content

Core idea

Foods contain organic constituents that can be identified using specific chemical or physical tests.

ConstituentTest / ProcedurePositive observationIndication
StarchAdd iodine solutionDark blueStarch is present
Reducing sugarAdd Fehling solution and heatBrick-red precipitateReducing sugar is present
LipidRub food on paperTranslucent spotLipid is present
ProteinAdd NaOH and CuSO₄: Biuret testVioletProtein is present

Negative results

Iodine gives brown-orange in negative results. Biuret and Fehling remain blue in negative results.

Bread investigation

Bread turns dark blue with iodine, so it contains starch. It turns violet with Biuret, so it contains protein. Heating bread produces water drops; silver nitrate after extraction gives white silver chloride precipitate, indicating mineral salts/chlorides. Thus, bread is a complex food.
